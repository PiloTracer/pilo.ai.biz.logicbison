# Verified sources — IT services strategy, job search, resume & interview

**Status:** Living catalog · **Needs:** re-verify anything older than its freshness window before it carries a decision · **Verified:** 2026-10-25.

This is the framework's source of record for three decisions the business skills keep having to make:

1. how IT/software services firms actually differentiate and price (**`@biz-strategy design`**, `@biz-pricing`, `concepts/it-services-strategy/prompt.md`);
2. how a job search actually produces conversations (**`@biz-career search`**);
3. what influences resume screening and interviews (**`@biz-career resume`** / `interview`).

**How to use it.** Cite the row, not the memory: quote the figure with its date and grade, and say what it does *not* prove. A claim that is not in this file and cannot be checked in the session is marked `Unverified` — it never carries a decision.

**Grades** are the framework ladder (`CONVENTIONS.md` § Evidence grades): **A** first-party/primary (the operator's own data, an author's own method page, a government or regulator guide) · **B** named research with a stated method and sample · **C** vendor/consultancy/media claim or single anecdote · **D** undated or unsourced — never decisive alone.

**Freshness** (`CONVENTIONS.md` § Freshness): prices, demand, platform mechanics and buyer behaviour are re-verified at 18 months (12 in fast-moving domains). Rows past that window are still useful for the *method*, not for the *number*.

## What the evidence does **not** support (read this before quoting anything)

1. **"ATS auto-rejects X% of resumes."** Every figure traced was vendor-published and unreachable for verification. The verified evidence is about **human attention** (a ~7.4 s first skim), not about automatic rejection. Do not cite an ATS-rejection percentage. Design for the skim, and keep the parseable layer simple; that is defensible from the attention data and from plain mechanics.
2. **"Referrals convert at N× job boards."** The peer-reviewed and vendor sources carrying the conversion tables were all paywalled or JavaScript-only at verification time. The *direction* (human contact beats volume) is supported by the AER result above plus the method sources; the **numbers** are not verified. Mark any such figure `Unverified` until the primary is read.
3. **"Positioning causes growth."** The Hinge study is correlational and self-published by a firm that sells branding. It supports "high-growth firms report differentiation as a priority", not "differentiate and you will grow".`@biz-strategy design` must not present it as causation.
4. **"Quantified achievements raise callback rates."** This is a reasonable inference from how structured assessment works (OPM) and from what screeners look at (Ladders), but no verified source here measures the callback effect. Treat it as a method rationale, marked `directional`, not a measured result.
